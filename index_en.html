How it works

The methodology in 4 steps

No unnecessary detours: this is how the simulator was built.

01
We prepared the data
We used international matches from 2010 onward to train the models and the full available history to calculate each team’s Elo.
02
We calculated each team’s level
We built our own Elo system: each team moves up or down based on its results, the opponent’s strength and the match importance.
03
We trained a goals model
The model estimates expected goals for each team based on its Elo, the opponent’s Elo and real home advantage.
04
We simulated the full World Cup
We ran 10,000 tournaments with the official bracket, exact best-third-place assignment through Annex C and progression through the final.
What stands out

Notable takeaways

🇧🇷
Brazil ranks lower than expected
With 2.9% probability, Brazil lands outside the top 10. The model weighs its current Elo more than its historical reputation.
🇲🇦
Morocco keeps a high probability
With 3.9%, Morocco ranks above Germany, the Netherlands and Brazil. Its strong recent cycle, including Qatar 2022, is reflected in its Elo.
🇯🇵 🇪🇨
Japan and Ecuador enter the top 10
Japan lands at 3.4% and Ecuador at 3.2%. They are teams with solid Elo ratings and less media noise than other contenders.
🇪🇸
Spain leads, but does not dominate completely
With 20.2%, Spain is the clear favorite. Even so, in almost 8 out of 10 simulations they do not become champion. That is exactly what a probability distribution shows.
